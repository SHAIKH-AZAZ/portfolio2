  1. Turn a schedule into demands

    Every row of a rebar schedule becomes a cut: a bar mark, a diameter, an exact length in millimetres. Nothing is eyeballed.

  2. Generate packings that fit

    A pattern is only legal if every piece on the bar fits — lengths plus one kerf per cut. Illegal patterns never enter the search.

  3. Pick the smallest tail

    Across all bars and patterns, choose the packing with the least off-cut — and among equal tails, fewer pieces, because every extra cut is one more chance to be off on site.

04 · how I work

The boring part that makes the rest hold up.

  1. Find the constraint before writing code

    Talk to whoever fights the problem daily. Write the hard limits down — 12 m stock, 4 mm kerf, a cover dimension — and treat them as law. Everything downstream obeys.

  2. Formulate first, type second

    Cast the messy job as math: lengths, counts, laps, wastage. If the formula can’t be checked by hand, it can’t be trusted in code.

  3. Ship the smallest real version

    One element type working end to end — parse, compute, export — beats half-finished everything. Then widen it.

  4. Verify like a site engineer will

    Numbers re-derived by hand, screens checked at 1440 and 390, no uncaught console errors. A schedule that’s off by a metre is a bad day on site.
